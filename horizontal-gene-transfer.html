How Does Horizontal Gene Transfer Change Our Understanding of Evolution?

The movement of genetic information between organisms and its role in the evolution of life

Independent Literature Review
Raees · Biological Sciences · Imperial College London

1. Introduction

Evolution is often described as the gradual change in populations over generations. One of the main ways genetic information is passed between organisms is through reproduction, where genetic material is inherited from parents by their offspring. This is known as vertical inheritance and forms an important part of how evolutionary relationships are understood.

However, genetic information can also move between organisms that are not directly related through reproduction. This process is known as horizontal gene transfer (HGT), or lateral gene transfer. Instead of genetic information being passed from parent to offspring, DNA can be transferred between different organisms. HGT is particularly common in bacteria and archaea and can allow organisms to acquire new genetic characteristics relatively quickly.

This review examines how horizontal gene transfer occurs, how it can affect bacterial evolution and how it has influenced our understanding of evolutionary relationships. It also considers whether the traditional idea of evolution as a branching tree is always sufficient to describe the history of life.

2. Vertical Inheritance and the Traditional Tree of Life

Most genetic inheritance occurs through reproduction. When an organism reproduces, its genetic information is passed to its offspring. Over many generations, mutations and natural selection can cause populations to become genetically different from one another.

This pattern of inheritance can be represented using an evolutionary tree. In a traditional evolutionary tree, each branch represents a lineage that has separated from a common ancestor. The further two organisms are separated on the tree, the more distant their evolutionary relationship is generally considered to be.

This model is useful because it provides a way of representing how species have changed and diversified over time. Darwin's theory of evolution by natural selection provided the foundation for understanding how populations can gradually change across generations.

However, the tree model mainly assumes that genetic information is passed vertically from ancestors to descendants. Horizontal gene transfer introduces another route by which genetic information can move between organisms.

This means that the history of some genes may not follow the same path as the history of the organisms that contain them.

3. What Is Horizontal Gene Transfer?

Horizontal gene transfer is the movement of genetic material between organisms without reproduction being involved. It is particularly important in bacteria and archaea, where genetic material can be transferred between cells that are not necessarily closely related.

The transferred DNA can provide the recipient organism with a new gene or a changed version of an existing gene. If the new genetic information provides an advantage, it may be maintained in the population through natural selection.

For example, a bacterium may acquire a gene that allows it to survive in an environment where it previously struggled to survive. If this characteristic increases its chances of survival and reproduction, bacteria carrying the gene may become more common.

HGT therefore provides another source of genetic variation. Mutations can create new genetic variation within a population, but horizontal gene transfer can introduce genetic information that already exists in another organism.

This can allow some organisms to acquire new characteristics without waiting for the same genetic change to arise through mutation.

4. How Does Horizontal Gene Transfer Occur?

There are three main mechanisms of horizontal gene transfer in bacteria: transformation, transduction and conjugation. These mechanisms differ in how DNA moves between cells.

Transformation

Transformation occurs when a bacterium takes up DNA that is present in its surrounding environment. DNA can be released into the environment when other bacterial cells die and break down.

Some bacteria are able to take up this DNA and, under suitable conditions, incorporate it into their own genetic material.

This means that genetic information released by one organism can potentially become part of the genome of another organism.

Transduction

Transduction involves viruses that infect bacteria, known as bacteriophages. During infection, a bacteriophage can accidentally package bacterial DNA and transfer it to another bacterial cell.

The second cell can then receive DNA from the original bacterium through the virus.

Although bacteriophages normally reproduce using bacterial cells, their ability to move bacterial DNA between cells means that they can also contribute to horizontal gene transfer.

Conjugation

Conjugation involves the direct transfer of DNA between bacterial cells. In many cases, the transferred DNA is found on a plasmid.

Plasmids are small, circular pieces of DNA that are separate from the main bacterial chromosome. Some plasmids contain genes that allow them to be transferred between cells.

During conjugation, a donor cell can transfer plasmid DNA to a recipient cell through a structure that connects the two cells. The recipient can then gain genes that were previously absent from its genetic material.

These three mechanisms provide bacteria with several ways of acquiring genetic information from other cells.

5. Horizontal Gene Transfer and Bacterial Evolution

Horizontal gene transfer can have an important effect on bacterial evolution because it allows bacteria to acquire genetic characteristics from other organisms.

If a transferred gene provides an advantage, natural selection can favour bacteria that possess it. Over time, the gene may become more common within a population.

For example, a gene that allows a bacterium to use a particular nutrient could provide an advantage in an environment where that nutrient is available. Similarly, a gene that allows bacteria to survive a harmful environmental condition could increase their chances of survival.

However, not every gene transferred between organisms will provide an advantage. Some transferred genes may have little effect, while others may be harmful to the recipient. Maintaining and using new genetic information can also require energy.

Therefore, horizontal gene transfer does not automatically lead to evolutionary success. The effect of a transferred gene depends on the environment and whether the new characteristic benefits the organism.

Research has found evidence of HGT across many bacterial genomes, although individual transfer events can have very different effects on bacterial populations.

6. Horizontal Gene Transfer and the Tree of Life

Horizontal gene transfer creates an important problem for the traditional evolutionary tree.

If a gene is passed vertically, its evolutionary history can generally be represented as part of the lineage of the organism. However, if that gene moves horizontally between organisms, its history may be different from the history of the organisms themselves.

For example, imagine that a gene moves from one bacterial species into another distantly related species. The recipient organism now contains genetic information that originated from a different evolutionary lineage.

If scientists studied only that gene, they could find that its evolutionary relationships do not match the relationships shown by other genes in the organism.

This means that the history of life cannot always be represented as a simple branching tree. In some cases, a network may provide a better representation because genetic information can move between branches.

This does not mean that the evolutionary tree is useless. Vertical inheritance remains an important part of evolution, and whole-genome studies continue to support many major evolutionary relationships. Instead, HGT shows that evolutionary history can be more complicated than a single tree suggests.

The idea of a network of life is therefore useful for understanding situations where genetic information has moved between different evolutionary lineages.

7. Limitations and Challenges in Studying HGT

Although horizontal gene transfer is well established, identifying individual HGT events can be difficult.

Scientists often compare DNA sequences between organisms to look for evidence that a gene may have been transferred. If a gene appears to have a very different evolutionary history from the rest of the genome, this can provide evidence for horizontal transfer.

However, genes can have similar sequences for several different reasons. For example, two organisms may have inherited a gene from a distant common ancestor, rather than one organism receiving it from the other through HGT.

This means that evidence for HGT needs to be interpreted carefully. Differences in gene history do not automatically prove that horizontal transfer occurred.

There is also variation in how important HGT is between different groups of organisms. HGT is particularly significant in bacteria and archaea, while its role in eukaryotes is more variable.

Therefore, HGT should not be viewed as replacing normal inheritance. Instead, it is another process that can contribute to genetic variation and evolution.

8. Conclusion

Horizontal gene transfer provides an additional way for genetic information to move between organisms. Unlike vertical inheritance, where genes are passed from parents to offspring, HGT allows genetic material to move between organisms that are not directly related through reproduction.

In bacteria, horizontal gene transfer can occur through transformation, transduction and conjugation. These mechanisms allow bacteria to acquire genetic information from their surroundings, from viruses or from other bacterial cells.

HGT can influence evolution by introducing new genetic characteristics into populations. When these characteristics provide an advantage, natural selection can increase their frequency.

Most importantly, horizontal gene transfer demonstrates that evolutionary history is not always completely represented by a simple branching tree. While vertical inheritance remains fundamental to evolution, the movement of genes between different lineages can create connections between branches.

Overall, horizontal gene transfer provides evidence that evolution can involve both the inheritance of genetic information through generations and the movement of genetic information between organisms. Understanding both processes gives a more complete picture of how genomes change and how the diversity of life has developed.
